\begin{center}
{\fontsize{18}{20}\bfseries ABSTRACT}
\end{center}

\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\justify
\noindent
Passive acoustic monitoring of Indian bird species faces a fundamental challenge that existing detectors
fail to address: BirdNET, trained predominantly on global temperate-zone recordings, misses a substantial
fraction of vocalisations from Indian species under realistic field-noise conditions. Applying a fixed
confidence threshold to its sigmoid-transformed logit outputs compounds this by conflating calibration
errors with genuine out-of-distribution (OOD) noise. This project presents a \textbf{noise-aware
machine-listening pipeline} that explicitly closes this gap through five tightly coupled components.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
The system begins with \textbf{subframe-level Noise Segregation V2}, which divides each 3-second
segment into six non-overlapping 0.5-second subframes and scores each using a weighted combination
of zero-crossing rate, spectral flatness, spectral centroid statistics, and insect-band autocorrelation.
A \textbf{Bird Guard} module subsequently checks for harmonic structure and spectral peak prominence
to recover bird-like segments misclassified by the noise scorer, and an \textbf{MLP-based Bird Rescue}
pass re-checks noise-labelled segments to recover faint or partially masked calls.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
Feature extraction uses frozen \textbf{BirdNET V2.4 TFLite embeddings} (1024-dimensional penultimate
global average pooling activations). A \textbf{focal-loss binary MLP classifier} (architecture:
$1024 \to 512 \to 256 \to 1$, $\gamma = 2.0$) is fine-tuned on these embeddings for
bird-versus-noise discrimination optimised for the Indian noise regime. An \textbf{autoencoder OOD
rejection gate} trained exclusively on bird embeddings uses per-sample reconstruction MSE to reject
out-of-distribution segments prior to MLP classification, with the gating threshold $\tau_{\mathrm{AE}}$
set at the 99th percentile of validation bird reconstruction errors.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
A calibrated \textbf{three-band router} ($\tau_{\mathrm{low}} = 0.3$, $\tau_{\mathrm{high}} = 0.7$)
with automated hard-example mining supports human-in-the-loop retraining cycles. On the iBC53 corpus
of 53 Indian bird species (10,044 three-second segments; 9,790 bird / 254 noise), the MLP-only
pipeline raises accuracy from 0.440 to \textbf{0.999} and F1 from 0.598 to \textbf{1.000}, reducing
the false-negative rate from 57.4\% to 0.04\% and the false-positive rate from 2.76\% to 1.97\%.
Adding the autoencoder gate yields accuracy 0.995 and F1 0.998 with a robust OOD safety layer.
ROC-AUC improves from 0.724 to 0.998. All improvements are statistically significant at $p < 0.05$
via paired $t$-test and Wilcoxon signed-rank test.

\vspace{0.5cm}

\noindent
\textbf{Keywords:} Bird Sound Classification, Bioacoustics, Noise Segregation V2, BirdNET V2.4,
Focal Loss, MLP Classifier, Autoencoder OOD Gating, Three-Band Routing, Active Learning,
iBC53, Indian Birds, Passive Acoustic Monitoring

\section{Introduction}

\noindent
India hosts over 1,300 bird species --- roughly 13\% of global avian diversity --- and ranks among the
world's twelve mega-biodiverse nations. Yet systematic monitoring of this diversity remains constrained
by terrain, cost, and the scarcity of trained ornithologists in remote areas. Passive acoustic monitoring
(PAM), which deploys low-cost recorders to capture soundscapes over days or weeks, has emerged as the
principal scalable alternative for avian biodiversity assessment.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
Indian soundscapes present an unusually severe noise regime: dense insect choruses (crickets, cicadas)
overlap spectrally with bird calls, monsoon rain generates broadband masking, agricultural activity
introduces anthropogenic interference, and recording equipment varies widely across citizen-science
deployments. These disturbances make accurate automated identification of bird vocalizations
particularly challenging.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
One of the most widely adopted systems for bird sound recognition is BirdNET~\cite{BirdNET}, a large-scale
deep learning model capable of identifying hundreds of bird species from raw audio recordings. However,
BirdNET was trained predominantly on North American and European recordings, and when deployed on Indian
field recordings, two systematic failure modes emerge. First, many endemic Indian species are absent or
under-represented in BirdNET's training data, causing the detector to assign near-zero confidence to
genuine vocalisations --- a high false-negative regime~\cite{WoodKahl}. Second, BirdNET confidence scores
are sigmoid-transformed logits rather than calibrated probabilities~\cite{WoodKahl}, meaning that a
universal threshold (typically 0.5) applied across species and soundscapes misclassifies noise-dominated
segments as birds, inflating false positives. Both failures directly degrade the curation quality of
training corpora for downstream species models.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
This project addresses these limitations by proposing a structured, \textbf{noise-aware pipeline} that
integrates subframe-level noise segregation with harmonic checking, MLP-based bird rescue, frozen BirdNET
V2.4 embedding extraction, a focal-loss MLP classifier, an autoencoder OOD rejection gate, and a
three-band confidence router with automated hard-example mining --- specifically targeting Indian species
and noise conditions.

\section{Motivation}

\noindent
The motivation for this project arises from two practical failure modes directly observed on the
iBC53 corpus~\cite{iBC53}. When BirdNET confidence thresholding was evaluated directly on iBC53-derived
segments, we observed accuracy of only 0.440 and a false-negative rate of 57.4\% --- confirming that
off-the-shelf thresholding is untenable for Indian PAM workflows. This catastrophic FNR means that
a PAM deployment surveying a forest patch would label more than half of genuine bird vocalisations
as noise, systematically under-counting species richness and activity levels.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
Another key motivation is the need for automation in large-scale data processing. Manual annotation
of bird sounds is time-consuming and requires significant domain expertise, making it impractical
when working with large volumes of passive acoustic data. The severe class imbalance in real-world
corpora (approximately 38.5:1 bird-to-noise ratio in iBC53 after pipeline processing) further
complicates classifier training and threshold selection.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
By combining structured subframe-level noise filtering with harmonic bird-guard checking, MLP-based
bird rescue, deep feature extraction using frozen BirdNET embeddings, focal-loss MLP classification,
autoencoder OOD gating, and three-band confidence routing, the system becomes robust and adaptable
to Indian field conditions while maintaining efficiency and scalability.

\section{Problem Statement}

\noindent
The primary problem addressed in this project is the reliable binary classification of audio segments
as bird-containing or noise-only in real-world Indian soundscapes. Existing systems fail in two
systematic ways: (1)~BirdNET's closed-set species confidence, when applied at a fixed threshold to
Indian recordings, misses 57.4\% of genuine bird vocalisations because many endemic Indian species
are absent from its training distribution; and (2)~BirdNET sigmoid outputs are uncalibrated~\cite{WoodKahl},
so noise-dominated segments receive inflated confidence scores and are misclassified as bird detections.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
This dual failure mode is compounded by the absence of an integrated pipeline that couples domain-specific
noise scoring with harmonic structure verification, a bird rescue mechanism for partially masked calls,
frozen embedding extraction, a domain-specific focal-loss classifier, OOD rejection gating, and
uncertainty-surfacing confidence routing. The lack of such integration leads to inconsistent performance
and limits practical applicability in Indian ecological monitoring tasks.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
Therefore, the problem is defined as: designing a reliable, noise-aware, and statistically validated
system that accurately separates bird vocalisations from background noise in Indian field recordings,
while maintaining modularity, efficiency, and scalability for real-world PAM deployment.
\newpage
\section{Objectives}

\noindent
The objectives of this project are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item To develop a preprocessing framework that resamples raw audio to 48~kHz mono, segments
          recordings into non-overlapping 3-second clips, and removes near-silent segments using
          RMS energy thresholding ($< -40$~dB).
    \item To design and implement \textbf{Noise Segregation V2} --- a six-subframe, multi-feature
          noise scoring module augmented with harmonic Bird Guard checking and an MLP Bird Rescue
          pass --- that effectively differentiates bird sounds from background noise prior to
          embedding extraction.
    \item To extract 1024-dimensional audio feature embeddings using the frozen BirdNET V2.4
          TFLite model~\cite{BirdNET} (penultimate global average pooling activation) for reproducible,
          high-quality feature representations.
    \item To build a focal-loss binary MLP classifier ($1024 \to 512 \to 256 \to 1$, $\gamma = 2.0$)
          trained on BirdNET embeddings that accurately predicts bird-versus-noise probability,
          with early stopping on validation F1 and threshold sweep for optimal operating point.
    \item To incorporate an \textbf{autoencoder OOD rejection gate} trained exclusively on
          bird embeddings, using per-sample reconstruction MSE with gating threshold
          $\tau_{\mathrm{AE}}$ set at the 99th percentile of validation bird reconstruction
          errors, to reject out-of-distribution inputs before classification.
    \item To construct an end-to-end pipeline integrating preprocessing, noise segregation,
          embedding extraction, classification, OOD gating, and three-band confidence routing
          ($\tau_{\mathrm{low}} = 0.3$, $\tau_{\mathrm{high}} = 0.7$) with automated
          hard-example mining for human-in-the-loop active learning.
    \item To evaluate the system via a three-way quantitative benchmark (BirdNET baseline,
          MLP-only, MLP$+$AE gate) on the iBC53 corpus~\cite{iBC53} with statistical significance
          testing using paired $t$-test and Wilcoxon signed-rank test at $p < 0.05$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Summary}

\noindent
This chapter introduced the context of passive acoustic monitoring of Indian bird species and
identified two systematic failure modes of BirdNET confidence thresholding: a catastrophically
high false-negative rate (57.4\%) due to domain mismatch, and uncalibrated sigmoid scores
causing false positives. The motivation was grounded in observed empirical failure on the iBC53
corpus, and the objectives articulate the five coupled pipeline components --- Noise Segregation V2
with Bird Guard and Bird Rescue, frozen BirdNET V2.4 embeddings, focal-loss MLP, autoencoder OOD
gate, and three-band router --- that together address these gaps.

\section{Overview}

\noindent
Passive acoustic monitoring has emerged as the dominant scalable approach for biodiversity monitoring,
with automated machine learning analysis now essential for processing the large volumes of data it
produces~\cite{Stowell}. Deep learning tools have accelerated adoption across taxa and ecosystems.
However, real-world audio recordings are often noisy and unstructured. Environmental sounds such as
wind, insects, rainfall, and human activity frequently overlap with bird calls, making accurate
classification challenging. Noise and poor-quality data significantly affect model performance,
leading to incorrect predictions and reduced reliability in practical applications.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
Modern approaches rely on deep learning techniques such as convolutional neural networks capable of
learning patterns directly from audio spectrograms. These models have shown strong performance in
large-scale bird species recognition tasks, but still face difficulties in highly noisy environments
--- particularly in tropical soundscapes where overlapping biological signals and anthropogenic
interference are far more severe than in the temperate habitats that dominate current training
corpora.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
A critical advancement has been the use of feature embeddings and transfer learning. Embeddings
extracted from pre-trained systems such as BirdNET~\cite{BirdNET} capture important acoustic
characteristics, improving classification accuracy and generalization across diverse datasets.
This approach is especially useful when working with geographically distinct or under-represented
audio collections. Building on these research insights, this project proposes a structured
noise-aware pipeline that couples subframe-level acoustic scoring, harmonic verification, MLP
rescue, frozen embedding extraction, focal-loss classification, OOD gating, and three-band routing
into a unified workflow.

\newpage
\section{Literature Survey}

\noindent
The field of bird sound classification and passive acoustic monitoring has seen significant
advancements through the application of deep learning and transfer learning techniques.
The survey below reviews key works that inform the design of the proposed system.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
\textbf{Bird Sound Recognition and BirdNET.}
Kahl et al.\ \cite{BirdNET} introduced BirdNET, a large-scale deep learning system for avian
diversity monitoring capable of identifying hundreds of bird species from audio recordings. The
work demonstrates that domain-specific data augmentation and structured preprocessing are critical
for handling noisy and overlapping vocalizations. A subsequent study by Wood and Kahl~\cite{WoodKahl}
cautions that BirdNET confidence scores are sigmoid-transformed logits and not calibrated
probabilities; applying a fixed detection threshold across environments routinely inflates both
false-positive and false-negative rates, motivating the need for post-processing pipelines such
as the one proposed here. A broader critical assessment of BirdNET's applications, performance,
and known pitfalls is provided by P\'{e}rez-Granados~\cite{PerezGranados}.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
\textbf{BirdNET Embeddings and Transfer Learning.}
McGinn et al.\ \cite{McGinn} demonstrate that BirdNET embeddings capture meaningful ecological
information beyond species labels and can be used effectively for downstream habitat and
biodiversity analysis. Ghani et al.\ \cite{Ghani} show that global birdsong embeddings derived
from pre-trained models enable superior transfer learning for bioacoustic classification tasks,
significantly outperforming models trained from scratch. M\"{u}ller et al.~\cite{Muller} demonstrate
that shallow fine-tuning of BirdNET embeddings generalises better to complex multi-species
soundscapes than knowledge distillation, and that dataset characteristics strongly modulate
transfer performance --- a finding that directly motivates the Indian-specific fine-tuning strategy
adopted here. Embedding-based approaches also enable direct estimation of call density in passive
recordings without relying on fixed confidence thresholds~\cite{McGinn}.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
\textbf{Noise Handling and Dataset Preparation.}
Xie et al.\ \cite{Xie} provide a comprehensive review of bioacoustic signal denoising methods,
covering classical signal processing techniques as well as modern deep learning approaches.
Their review confirms that background noise remains a primary factor in degrading model
performance. Standard bioacoustic preprocessing applies bandpass filtering, spectral subtraction,
or energy-based segment gating, but these heuristics are manually tuned per site and treat
preprocessing as a binary pass/fail gate with no mechanism to recover bird calls partially
masked by noise~\cite{Stowell}. The iBC53 dataset used in this project, comprising passive
acoustic recordings from an Indian urban landscape spanning 53 species, is described by
Gupta et al.~\cite{iBC53}, providing ecologically grounded evaluation data.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
\textbf{Hybrid Rule-Based and Learned Screening.}
Hand-crafted features --- zero-crossing rate, spectral flatness, centroid stability --- reliably
distinguish tonal bird calls from broadband noise or insect choruses. When combined with deep
embedding classifiers, hybrid architectures offer interpretable, lightweight detection. Prior work
has not documented the combination of subframe-level acoustic voting with BirdNET embeddings for
Indian conditions. The Noise Segregation V2 module proposed here operationalises this hybrid
approach through a weighted six-subframe voting scheme with harmonic structure checking.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
\textbf{Out-of-Distribution Detection in Bioacoustics.}
Standard classifiers fail under distribution shift by assigning overconfident probabilities to
out-of-distribution noise. Ntalampiras and Potamitis~\cite{Ntalampiras} demonstrate
reconstruction-error-based methods for detecting unknown bird species and anomalous acoustic
events using autoencoder networks trained on known-class embeddings, where high reconstruction
error signals out-of-distribution inputs. This principle directly motivates the autoencoder OOD
rejection gate in the present system. The theoretical basis for autoencoder-based dimensionality
reduction and anomaly detection is established by Hinton and Salakhutdinov~\cite{Hinton}.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
\textbf{Active Learning for Bioacoustics.}
Human annotation of PAM recordings is cost-prohibitive at scale. Kath et al.\ \cite{Kath} show
that combining transfer learning with active learning significantly reduces annotation effort in
passive acoustic monitoring. Rauch et al.\ \cite{Rauch} develop deep active learning strategies
specifically for avian bioacoustics, demonstrating that uncertainty-based sample selection improves
classifier calibration over iterative annotation cycles. Dumoulin et al.\ \cite{Dumoulin} further
demonstrate that pre-computing embeddings and applying lightweight active learning classifiers
allows rapid model adaptation with minimal expert effort. The three-band router proposed here
concentrates uncertainty into an explicitly surfaced middle band, and automated hard-example
mining exports curated sets of likely false positives and false negatives for expert review.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
\textbf{Foundation Models and Loss Functions.}
Pre-trained embedding models represent the current state of the art for bioacoustic
classification~\cite{Stowell,Ghani}. The focal loss function introduced by Lin et al.\ \cite{FocalLoss}
addresses class imbalance during classifier training by down-weighting easily classified examples;
it is adopted here to improve sensitivity given the severe 38.5:1 class imbalance in the iBC53
corpus.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
Overall, the surveyed works collectively establish that deep learning and embedding-based transfer
learning are highly effective for bird sound classification, while identifying persistent challenges
around noise robustness and threshold calibration. The proposed system builds directly on these
findings by coupling Noise Segregation V2 with Bird Guard and Bird Rescue, frozen BirdNET
embeddings~\cite{BirdNET}, focal-loss MLP classification~\cite{FocalLoss}, autoencoder OOD
gating~\cite{Ntalampiras,Hinton}, and three-band routing with active learning~\cite{Kath,Rauch}
into a unified noise-aware pipeline specifically targeting Indian conditions.

\section{Methodologies}

\noindent
The proposed bird sound classification system follows a structured methodology integrating six tightly
coupled components: (1)~audio conditioning and RMS silence rejection; (2)~subframe-level Noise
Segregation V2 with harmonic Bird Guard; (3)~MLP-based Bird Rescue for faint or masked calls;
(4)~frozen BirdNET V2.4 embedding extraction~\cite{BirdNET}; (5)~focal-loss binary MLP
classification~\cite{FocalLoss}; and (6)~autoencoder OOD rejection gating~\cite{Ntalampiras,Hinton}
with three-band confidence routing and active learning support.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
Recordings are converted to mono and resampled to 48~kHz (BirdNET V2.4 requirement), then
partitioned into non-overlapping 3-second segments. Segments with RMS below $-40$~dB are discarded
as near-silent. Each surviving segment is divided into six non-overlapping 0.5-second subframes,
and five acoustic features are extracted per subframe: zero-crossing rate, spectral flatness,
spectral centroid mean and standard deviation, and an insect-band autocorrelation periodicity
flag. A weighted noise score determines each subframe's vote, and a majority vote across subframes
labels the segment.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
Before routing a segment to the noise class, a \textbf{Bird Guard} module checks for harmonic
structure (harmonic ratio $> 0.3$) and spectral peak prominence (peak-to-median ratio $> 3.0$).
Any segment passing either check is retained in the bird class despite a noise-majority vote.
Segments labelled as noise and failing the Bird Guard undergo a \textbf{Bird Rescue} pass using
a lightweight MLP; those with $P(\mathrm{bird}) \geq \tau_{\mathrm{rescue}}$ are recovered
to the bird class.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
Surviving segments are encoded by frozen BirdNET V2.4 via TFLite inference to yield
1024-dimensional embeddings. A focal-loss binary MLP ($1024 \to 512 \to 256 \to 1$) is trained
on these embeddings. An autoencoder OOD gate trained exclusively on bird embeddings uses
per-sample reconstruction MSE with threshold $\tau_{\mathrm{AE}}$ at the 99th percentile of
validation bird errors to reject out-of-distribution segments. The three-band router
($\tau_{\mathrm{low}} = 0.3$, $\tau_{\mathrm{high}} = 0.7$) surfaces uncertain segments for
human-in-the-loop active learning.

\section{Mathematical Model}

\noindent
The proposed system is represented mathematically as a multi-stage pipeline consisting of
preprocessing, noise segregation, feature extraction, classification, OOD gating, and
three-band decision routing.

\subsection{Audio Segmentation}

\noindent
Let the input audio signal be represented as $X = \{x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n\}$. The signal is
resampled to 48~kHz mono and divided into fixed-length 3-second non-overlapping segments:
\[
    X \rightarrow \{S_1, S_2, \ldots, S_k\}
\]
Any segment whose RMS energy falls below $-40$~dB is discarded before further processing.

\subsection{Noise Segregation V2}

\noindent
Each segment $S_j$ is divided into six non-overlapping 0.5-second subframes. For each subframe
$f_i$, five acoustic features are extracted and combined into a weighted noise score:
\[
    \mathrm{Score}(f_i) = w_{\mathrm{zcr}}\,\widehat{\mathrm{ZCR}}
                      + w_{\mathrm{flat}}\,\widehat{\mathrm{Flat}}
                      + w_{\mathrm{cflag}}\,\mathrm{CFlag}
                      + w_{\mathrm{cstd}}\,\hat{\sigma}_c
                      + w_{\mathrm{ins}}\,\mathrm{IFlag}
\]
where $\widehat{\mathrm{ZCR}}$ is the normalised zero-crossing rate, $\widehat{\mathrm{Flat}}$
is the spectral flatness, $\mathrm{CFlag}$ and $\hat{\sigma}_c$ capture spectral centroid
behaviour, and $\mathrm{IFlag}$ is an insect-band autocorrelation indicator. The weights
$(w_{\mathrm{zcr}}, w_{\mathrm{flat}}, w_{\mathrm{cflag}}, w_{\mathrm{cstd}}, w_{\mathrm{ins}})
= (0.25, 0.30, 0.15, 0.15, 0.15)$ reflect the relative discriminative power of each feature
in Indian soundscapes.

If $\mathrm{Score}(f_i)$ exceeds a configurable threshold, the subframe casts a noise vote;
otherwise it is treated as bird-like. The segment label is determined by majority vote:
\[
    \mathrm{Label}(S_j) = \operatorname{mode}\bigl(\mathrm{vote}(f_1), \ldots, \mathrm{vote}(f_6)\bigr)
\]

\noindent
\textbf{Bird Guard.} Before routing a noise-majority segment to the noise class, two acoustic
signatures specific to bird vocalisations are checked: (a)~harmonic structure (harmonic ratio
$> 0.3$), and (b)~spectral peak prominence (peak-to-median ratio $> 3.0$). Any segment passing
either check is retained in the bird class.

\subsection{Bird Rescue}

\noindent
Segments labelled as noise by Noise Segregation V2 and failing the Bird Guard undergo a
second-chance classification using a lightweight MLP trained on the same embedding space.
If the MLP assigns $P(\mathrm{bird}) \geq \tau_{\mathrm{rescue}}$, the segment is rescued
to its species folder.

\subsection{Feature Extraction (BirdNET V2.4 Embedding)}

\noindent
Each bird-labelled segment surviving the above stages is passed through the frozen BirdNET
V2.4 TFLite model~\cite{BirdNET}. The penultimate global average pooling activation yields:
\[
    \mathbf{e}_j = \phi(S_j), \quad \mathbf{e}_j \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}
\]

\subsection{Focal-Loss MLP Classifier}

\noindent
The embedding is passed through an MLP with architecture $1024 \to 512 \to 256 \to 1$,
with batch normalisation, ReLU, and dropout ($p = 0.3$) after each hidden layer, and
sigmoid output activation. Training minimises the focal loss~\cite{FocalLoss}:
\[
    \mathcal{L} = -\alpha\,(1-p)^{\gamma}\,y\log(p)
                  - (1-\alpha)\,p^{\gamma}\,(1-y)\log(1-p)
\]
with $\gamma = 2.0$.

\subsection{Autoencoder OOD Rejection Gate}

\noindent
A symmetric bottleneck autoencoder ($1024 \to 128 \to 1024$)~\cite{Hinton} is trained
exclusively on bird-only embeddings. The per-sample reconstruction MSE is~\cite{Ntalampiras}:
\[
    \varepsilon(\mathbf{e}_j) = \frac{1}{1024}\sum_{i=1}^{1024}
                                \bigl(e_{ji} - \hat{e}_{ji}\bigr)^2
\]
The gating threshold $\tau_{\mathrm{AE}}$ is set at the 99th percentile of reconstruction
errors on validation bird embeddings (empirically $\tau_{\mathrm{AE}} = 0.16975$ on iBC53).

\subsection{Three-Band Decision Function and Active Learning}

\noindent
Final classification uses two fixed deployment thresholds:
\[
    \text{Output} =
    \begin{cases}
        \text{Bird},      & p \geq \tau_{\mathrm{high}} = 0.7 \\
        \text{Noise},     & p \leq \tau_{\mathrm{low}}  = 0.3 \\
        \text{Uncertain}, & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
\]

$\tau_{\mathrm{high}} = 0.7$ into confident bird, confident noise, or uncertain bands.

\section{Algorithms}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Preprocessing, Noise Segregation V2, Bird Guard, and Bird Rescue}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Load raw audio files from the iBC53 corpus
\STATE Resample all recordings to 48~kHz mono
\STATE Segment each recording into non-overlapping 3-second clips
\FOR{each segment $S_j$}
    \STATE Compute RMS energy of $S_j$
    \IF{RMS energy $<$ $-40$~dB}
        \STATE Discard segment (near-silent)
    \ELSE
        \STATE Divide $S_j$ into six 0.5-second subframes
        \FOR{each subframe $f_i$}
            \STATE Extract ZCR, spectral flatness, spectral centroid (mean \& std), insect-band autocorrelation (IFlag)
            \STATE Compute weighted score:
                   $\mathrm{Score}(f_i) = 0.25\,\widehat{\mathrm{ZCR}}
                   + 0.30\,\widehat{\mathrm{Flat}}
                   + 0.15\,\mathrm{CFlag}
                   + 0.15\,\hat{\sigma}_c
                   + 0.15\,\mathrm{IFlag}$
            \IF{$\mathrm{Score}(f_i) >$ configurable threshold}
                \STATE Subframe votes \textit{noise}
            \ELSE
                \STATE Subframe votes \textit{bird}
            \ENDIF
        \ENDFOR
        \STATE Apply majority vote: $\mathrm{Label}(S_j) = \operatorname{mode}(\mathrm{vote}(f_1),\ldots,\mathrm{vote}(f_6))$
        \IF{$\mathrm{Label}(S_j) = \text{noise}$}
            \STATE \textbf{Bird Guard}: check harmonic ratio $> 0.3$ \textbf{OR} peak-to-median ratio $> 3.0$
            \IF{either check passes}
                \STATE Override label to \textit{bird} $\to$ route to species folder
            \ELSE
                \STATE \textbf{Bird Rescue}: run lightweight MLP; compute $P(\mathrm{bird})$
                \IF{$P(\mathrm{bird}) \geq \tau_{\mathrm{rescue}}$}
                    \STATE Rescue segment $\to$ species folder
                \ELSE
                    \STATE Confirm \textit{noise} $\to$ noise corpus
                \ENDIF
            \ENDIF
        \ELSE
            \STATE Route to species folder
        \ENDIF
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\vspace{0.4cm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{BirdNET V2.4 Embedding Extraction}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Load frozen BirdNET V2.4 TFLite model~\cite{BirdNET}
\FOR{each bird-labelled segment $S_j$}
    \STATE Extract penultimate global average pooling activation:
           $\mathbf{e}_j = \phi(S_j) \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}$
    \STATE Store embedding with metadata in HDF5 manifest
\ENDFOR
\STATE Build stratified train/validation/test split (75\% / 15\% / 10\%)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\vspace{0.4cm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Focal-Loss MLP Classifier Training}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Load embeddings and binary labels (bird=1, noise=0)
\STATE Initialize MLP: $1024 \to 512 \to 256 \to 1$ with batch normalisation, ReLU, dropout ($p=0.3$)
\FOR{each epoch (up to 50)}
    \STATE Forward pass; compute sigmoid output probability $p$
    \STATE Compute focal loss~\cite{FocalLoss} with $\gamma=2.0$:
           $\mathcal{L} = -\alpha(1-p)^{2}y\log p
           - (1-\alpha)p^{2}(1-y)\log(1-p)$
    \STATE Backpropagate and update weights (Adam optimizer, cosine-annealing schedule)
    \IF{validation F1 does not improve for 7 consecutive epochs}
        \STATE Early stopping
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE Sweep 50 candidate thresholds on validation set; save F1-optimal threshold
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\vspace{0.4cm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Autoencoder OOD Gate Training (P99 Threshold)}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Select bird-only embeddings from training split
\STATE Initialize symmetric autoencoder ($1024 \to 128 \to 1024$)~\cite{Hinton}
\FOR{each epoch (up to 30)}
    \STATE Encode embedding $\mathbf{e}_j$; reconstruct $\hat{\mathbf{e}}_j$
    \STATE Compute MSE loss: $\varepsilon = \frac{1}{1024}\|\mathbf{e}_j - \hat{\mathbf{e}}_j\|^2$
    \STATE Update weights
\ENDFOR
\STATE Set $\tau_{\mathrm{AE}}$ = P99 of reconstruction errors on validation bird embeddings
       ($\tau_{\mathrm{AE}} = 0.16975$ on iBC53)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\vspace{0.4cm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Inference with Three-Band Routing and OOD Gate}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\FOR{each input segment}
    \STATE Extract embedding $\mathbf{e}_j = \phi(S_j)$
    \IF{autoencoder gate enabled}
        \STATE Compute $\varepsilon(\mathbf{e}_j) = \frac{1}{1024}\|\mathbf{e}_j - \hat{\mathbf{e}}_j\|^2$
        \IF{$\varepsilon(\mathbf{e}_j) > \tau_{\mathrm{AE}} = 0.16975$}
            \STATE Route to \textbf{Noise} (OOD rejection); \textbf{skip} MLP
        \ENDIF
    \ENDIF
    \STATE Compute MLP probability $p$
    \IF{$p \geq \tau_{\mathrm{high}} = 0.7$}
        \STATE Route to \textbf{Confident Bird}
    \ELSIF{$p \leq \tau_{\mathrm{low}} = 0.3$}
        \STATE Route to \textbf{Confident Noise}
    \ELSE
        \STATE Route to \textbf{Uncertain} $\rightarrow$ human review queue
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\vspace{0.4cm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Active Learning and Hard-Example Mining}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Scan uncertain-band and noise output directories for candidate false positives/negatives
\STATE Export curated sets for expert annotation~\cite{Kath,Rauch}
\STATE Update dataset with corrected labels
\STATE Retrain MLP (and autoencoder if needed) on augmented dataset
\STATE Repeat until performance converges on held-out evaluation
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Summary}

\noindent
This chapter presented the complete methodology of the proposed noise-aware bird sound
classification system. The pipeline encompasses audio preprocessing with RMS-based silence
removal, Noise Segregation V2 with six-subframe weighted acoustic voting, harmonic Bird Guard
checking, MLP-based Bird Rescue for faint or masked calls, frozen BirdNET V2.4 embedding
extraction (1024-D)~\cite{BirdNET}, focal-loss MLP training ($\gamma = 2.0$)~\cite{FocalLoss},
P99-thresholded autoencoder OOD rejection gating ($\tau_{\mathrm{AE}} = 0.16975$)~\cite{Ntalampiras,Hinton},
three-band decision routing ($\tau_{\mathrm{low}} = 0.3$, $\tau_{\mathrm{high}} = 0.7$), and
active learning with automated hard-example mining~\cite{Kath,Rauch}.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Dataset: iBC53}

\noindent
The iBC53 corpus~\cite{iBC53} contains recordings of 53 Indian bird species gathered under varying
urban and peri-urban conditions around Bengaluru. After resampling, segmentation (3\,s non-overlapping),
RMS silence removal, Noise Segregation V2, Bird Guard, and Bird Rescue, the pipeline yields
\textbf{9,790 bird-labelled} and \textbf{254 noise-labelled} segments (10,044 total). The severe
class imbalance ($\approx 38.5{:}1$) motivates the use of focal loss~\cite{FocalLoss}.

\subsection{Splits and Evaluation Protocol}

\noindent
Stratified sampling divides the corpus 75\%/15\%/10\% into train, validation, and test sets,
preserving class balance in each split. The BirdNET confidence baseline~\cite{BirdNET} is evaluated
on the full manifest by assigning the maximum per-segment species detection confidence and thresholding at 0.5.

\subsection{Baselines and Ablations}

\noindent
Three systems are compared: (1)~\textbf{BirdNET Baseline}~\cite{BirdNET}: maximum species
confidence $\geq 0.5$; (2)~\textbf{Pipeline (MLP Only)}: frozen BirdNET embeddings through the
trained focal-loss MLP~\cite{FocalLoss} at probability threshold 0.5; (3)~\textbf{Pipeline $+$ AE Gate}:
autoencoder OOD rejection~\cite{Ntalampiras,Hinton} ($\tau_{\mathrm{AE}} = 0.16975$, P99) preceding the MLP.

\subsection{Computational Environment}

\noindent
All experiments run on commodity CPU hardware without a dedicated GPU. BirdNET inference is
executed via the TFLite runtime; MLP and autoencoder training use PyTorch. The pipeline is
cross-platform and reproducible via a single \texttt{config.yaml} entry point.

False-Positive Rate (FPR), False-Negative Rate (FNR), ROC-AUC, and PR-AUC.

\subsection{Qualitative Error Analysis}

\noindent
The BirdNET baseline's 5,615 false negatives predominantly arise from: (1)~Indian-endemic species
absent from BirdNET's training data~\cite{WoodKahl}; (2)~forest recordings with dense insect chorus

\subsection{Active Learning and Hard-Example Mining}

\noindent
Over a typical deployment cycle, the three-band router routes $\approx 2$\% of segments into
the uncertain band. Annotated corrections feed back into training, tightening both the classifier
and the underlying corpus~\cite{Dumoulin}.

\subsection{Discussion}

\noindent
The results demonstrate that the proposed pipeline represents a fundamental improvement over
confidence-thresholded BirdNET for Indian PAM workflows. The dramatic FNR reduction
(57.4\%$\to$0.04\%) has direct practical consequences: under the BirdNET baseline, more than
half of genuine bird vocalisations are labeled as noise, systematically under-counting species
richness. All components run on commodity CPU hardware, enabling edge deployment on low-power
embedded recorders.

\section{Conclusion}

\noindent
This project has presented a noise-aware machine-listening pipeline for Indian bird sound
classification that combines six tightly coupled components: RMS silence rejection,
subframe-level Noise Segregation V2 with harmonic Bird Guard checking, MLP-based Bird Rescue,
frozen BirdNET V2.4 embeddings~\cite{BirdNET}, a focal-loss binary MLP classifier~\cite{FocalLoss},
and a P99-thresholded autoencoder OOD rejection gate~\cite{Ntalampiras,Hinton}, together with a
three-band confidence router and automated hard-example mining~\cite{Kath,Rauch}.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
On the iBC53 corpus~\cite{iBC53} of 53 Indian species (10,044 three-second segments), the
MLP-only pipeline raises accuracy from 0.440 to \textbf{0.999} and F1 from 0.598 to \textbf{1.000},
reducing FNR from 57.4\% to 0.04\% and improving ROC-AUC from 0.724 to 0.998. Adding the
autoencoder OOD gate yields accuracy 0.995 and F1 0.998 with robust OOD rejection. All improvements
are statistically significant at $p < 0.05$.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
These results confirm that a structured, noise-aware pipeline with domain-specific fine-tuning
substantially outperforms single-threshold approaches in Indian PAM scenarios, and provides a
practical foundation for field-deployable bioacoustic monitoring across India's diverse soundscapes.

\section{Future Scope}

\noindent
The current system performs well in classifying bird sounds from Indian urban and peri-urban
soundscapes, but there is meaningful scope to extend its applicability. One important direction
is to evaluate and adapt the system across a wider variety of environmental conditions, including
recordings from different geographical regions, seasonal periods, and recording hardware configurations.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
Another area for improvement is expansion beyond binary bird-versus-noise classification to
multi-species identification. Incorporating more labeled audio from underrepresented endemic
species and acoustically challenging environments would reduce sensitivity to distribution shift.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
The current implementation processes segments independently without exploiting temporal
continuity. Incorporating temporal context --- via transformer-based audio encoders or recurrent
classifiers --- could further improve performance on short or atypical calls. Making the Noise
Segregation V2 feature weights learnable would make the noise segregation stage more transferable
across regions and sensor types.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
Finally, expanding the noise corpus with real-world recordings from diverse Indian environments
would improve generalization of both the focal-loss MLP and the autoencoder OOD
